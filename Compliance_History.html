Before We Get Into Pricing

Dodd-Frank & the Durbin Amendment

Dual Pricing didn't start as a payments product, it started at the gas pump. Here's how a decades-old workaround for surcharge restrictions turned into the compliant model you sell today, and why compliance has been the deciding factor at every step along the way.

1970s
During the oil crisis, gas stations pioneer cash discounting, posting a lower price for cash to offset rising card costs and protect thin fuel margins. This is where the practice starts, a workaround at the pump, not a product.
1981
The Cash Discount Act amends Truth in Lending, legally drawing the line between a "discount" and a "surcharge." It turns the gas-station workaround into a codified, compliant model, the direct root of today's Dual Pricing.
1984
The original federal surcharge ban lapses. Surcharging becomes a state-by-state patchwork while cash discounting stays legal nationwide, which is exactly why it became the dominant path for the next three decades.
2010
The Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform Act passes. Tucked inside it: the Durbin Amendment, capping debit interchange for large banks and mandating competitive debit routing, reshaping the cost pressure every pricing program is built to offset.
2013
A Visa/Mastercard settlement opens the door nationally for credit-only surcharge programs, giving merchants a second compliant path alongside cash discounting.
Today
Compliant POS technology, BIN lookup, card-price-as-posted-price systems, automates what gas stations once did by hand. What started as a pump-side workaround is now the Dual Pricing program you sell.
Bottom line: cash discounting was a compliance workaround before it was ever a sales pitch. That's still true today, every Dual Pricing rule traces back to staying on the legal side of the discount versus surcharge line.
