Cost shifting is not a fourth pricing model.

Surcharging, cash discount and dual pricing change how a merchant presents costs to customers. They sit on top of the pricing model in your proposal, and each one has its own structure, display requirements and compliance review.

Credit cards only

Surcharge

A disclosed fee added to eligible credit card transactions. Debit and prepaid cards are never surcharged, even when processed without a PIN.

What the merchant must display

Posted prices

The base price. Some states, such as New York, also require the total card price to be shown.

Store entrance

A sign stating that a surcharge is added to credit card transactions.

Point of sale

A sign showing the surcharge percentage and that it applies to credit cards only.

Receipt

The surcharge on its own line with the dollar amount.

Online or phone

The surcharge disclosed before the customer completes payment.

A discount for cash

Cash Discount

The posted price is the card price, and customers who pay with cash receive a discount. It is always a discount for cash, never a fee for using a card.

What the merchant must display

Posted prices

The card price.

Store entrance

A sign stating that a discount is available for paying with cash.

Point of sale

A sign showing the discount percentage and that it applies to cash only.

Receipt

The cash discount on its own line, shown as a negative dollar amount.

Two prices, customer chooses

Dual Pricing

Every item carries two prices, one for cash and one for card, so the customer sees both before choosing how to pay.

What the merchant must display

Posted prices

Both a cash price and a card price on every item, shelf or menu.

Store entrance

A sign stating that prices differ based on payment method.

Point of sale

Both prices visible at checkout before the customer pays.

Receipt

The price charged, based on how the customer paid.
