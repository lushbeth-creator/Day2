Before We Get Into Pricing

Compliance & History: Dodd-Frank & the Durbin Amendment

Your eLearning course covers this in depth, so here's the short version: three federal actions, spread across three decades, are the reason the pricing models coming up next look the way they do today.

1981
Cash Discount Act amends Truth in Lending to let merchants offer a discount for cash, while drawing a legal line between a "discount" and a "surcharge." This is the root of today's Dual Pricing model.
2010
Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform Act passes in response to the 2008 financial crisis. Tucked inside it: the Durbin Amendment, aimed specifically at debit card interchange.
2011-12
Durbin takes effect. It caps debit interchange for banks over $10B in assets and requires every debit card to route over at least two unaffiliated networks, giving merchants routing choice for the first time.
2013
A merchant class-action settlement with Visa and Mastercard opens the door for merchants to add a credit-only surcharge, subject to card network rules and applicable state law. The Surcharge Program you sell today grew out of this.

Why This Matters for IC+

Durbin only caps debit interchange. Credit interchange remains set by the networks and varies by card type, which is exactly why card mix conversations matter on IC+ deals.

Why Debit Is Always Excluded

Durbin's debit routing and fee protections are federal law. That's the underlying reason surcharge and dual pricing programs are built to exclude debit and prepaid automatically.

Bottom line: you don't need the legal history day to day, but knowing debit is federally protected and credit is not explains almost every compliance rule in the pricing models that follow.
