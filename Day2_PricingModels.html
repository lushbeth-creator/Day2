Same cost stack, different presentation.

Every proposal starts with understanding how a merchant is priced today. When you know how flat rate, tiered and interchange-plus pricing each handle the same underlying costs, you can read a merchant's statement with confidence, explain the difference clearly and build a proposal that fits how they actually process.

Simple and predictable

Flat Rate

One fixed percentage, often with a per-transaction fee, applies regardless of card type. The provider absorbs variation in the underlying cost and presents one easy-to-understand price.

Best Fit

Merchants prioritizing simplicity and fast setup

Strength

Predictable costs and easy reconciliation

Watch For

May cost more for high volume or debit-heavy businesses

Advisor lens: Simple does not automatically mean inexpensive. Lower-cost transactions are not separately passed through to the merchant.

Buckets based on qualification

Tiered Pricing

Transactions are grouped into qualified, mid-qualified and non-qualified tiers. The rate depends on card type, rewards level and how the transaction was entered.

Best Fit

Legacy arrangements where bucket pricing is already established

Strength

Easy headline rates for sales conversations

Watch For

Limited transparency and unexpected non-qualified costs

Advisor lens: A low qualified rate does not tell the whole story. Merchants need to understand where their actual transactions land.

Cost plus transparent markup

Interchange+

The merchant pays the actual interchange and network costs plus a separately stated processor or acquirer markup. The statement shows cost drivers instead of blending them into one rate.

Best Fit

Larger or more sophisticated merchants seeking visibility

Strength

Transparent costs and direct benefit from lower interchange

Watch For

More complex statements and reconciliation

Advisor lens: Transparency creates a better optimization conversation, but the merchant must be comfortable reading a more detailed statement.
